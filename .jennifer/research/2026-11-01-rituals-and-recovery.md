# Facet C · Rituals and workflow structure (day / week / quarter / projects)

2026-11-01. Grades: A meta-analysis/RCT · B single peer-reviewed or reputable survey · C practitioner/vendor/book · D anecdote. _(secondary)_ = figure seen only in a secondary source. Builds on `research/2026-10-23-productivity-methods-evidence.md` (not repeated here: implementation intentions, self-monitoring d+ = .40, goal setting, planning fallacy, Masicampo, Zeigarnik, end-of-day reflection, WIP/Kanban).

**Bottom line:** no study tests a weekly review, a daily planning ritual or a quarter cycle as such. The evidence that does exist covers the parts: plan daily, use near-term subgoals, keep few goals, and let go of stuck ones. It says less about building better plans than about cleaning up when plans fail. The current spec covers reflection well but has no step for clearing the backlog and no rule for letting go of goals. Add both before adding anything else.

## 2. The minimal effective loop

Design rule from #1, #3, #14: plan short horizons concretely and review against actuals. The weekly review is where cleanup happens, so keep the daily loop tiny.

### Day: 3–5 min plan + ≤1 min check-in

1. **Decide yesterday's leftovers** (≤1 min). Each unfinished task gets Today, a date, Someday or Drop. Bulk actions only. Never roll tasks over silently (#5–#8).
2. **Pick ≤3 "must" tasks**, preferring ones linked to this week's outcomes (#14 proximal subgoals; prior research on the mere-urgency effect).
3. **Fit check.** Total estimates vs free hours left after fixed events. Warn when over, using the user's own estimate history (#3).
4. **Block or assign a time of day** for the ≤3 musts (prior research: implementation intentions). Rough blocks such as "morning" are allowed.
5. **On interrupt-heavy days**, offer one "if X comes up, then I'll…" line (#1 contingent planning).
6. **Evening check-in** (as specced): 1-tap feeling + optional note. Add an optional "shape tomorrow" link that adds tasks to tomorrow (#2). Morning still commits the plan; no evidence picks night over morning.

### Week: 10–15 min, one sitting, any day the user chooses

1. **Feel** (5 s).
2. **Outcomes** with evidence → Achieved / Not yet / Dropped (#18).
3. **Clear the deck** (Get clear/current, #9): inbox count, overdue tasks, tasks rolled ≥ N times, projects with no open task or no activity in 4+ weeks. Bulk triage: reschedule / Someday / drop / pause project.
4. **One change**: a single reflect prompt, with last week's change shown above it.
5. **Plan**: next week's fixed commitments shown first (#12), then ≤3 outcomes (pre-filled with Not yet ones), each with a first task and day.
6. **Done.**

Show the numbers (plan vs tracked, habits) **inside** steps 2 and 5 where they bear on a decision, not as a separate read-only page.

### Quarter: 30–45 min at weeks 13/1, plus a 5-min check at week 6–7

- **Wrap-up**: per priority, achieved outcomes; then each priority is **Keep / Change / Drop**. Unfinished priorities are not pre-filled (#18).
- **Plan**: ≤3 priorities, each with a first outcome for week 1 (#14).
- **Midpoint check** inside that week's review: one line per priority, "on track / at risk / drop". This is Wodtke's confidence idea (#11) applied to a single person.
- **Optional week 13 as cool-down**, Shape Up style (#20): no new outcomes; clean up and plan the next quarter.

## 3. Failure and recovery design

| Failure | Design | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Overloaded Today | Fit check with a capacity bar; "move the rest" sends overflow to a date or Someday | #3; prior planning-fallacy research |
| Silent rollover pile-up ("overdue debt") | No silent rollover. Show a rollover count on each task (like Sunsama). At N = 3 rollovers, the morning step asks "Reschedule / Someday / Drop". | #5, #6 |
| Overdue backlog after a break | **Reset** action: tasks overdue more than 7 days go into one list with "Reschedule to…", "Move to Someday" and "Drop all" (undoable). Frame it as a fresh start, not failure. | #7, #8; no direct research (gap) |
| Missed weekly review | The card stays until Wednesday. After that, offer a **2-min quick review**: feel, outcome statuses, next week's ≤3. With 2+ weeks missed, review only the latest week; mark the rest "not reviewed", without guilt. | #9 "7–10 days"; spec risk "flow feels long" |
| Outcome "Not yet" again and again | After 2 carries, require a decision: shrink scope (Shape Up appetite), set a new date, or drop. Show "carried ×2". | #18 action crisis; #20 circuit breaker |
| Too many active projects | Soft cap, e.g. 5 active projects, with an "Active / Paused / Someday" state. The weekly deck-clearing step flags stale projects. | #19, #21 (C only) |
| Goals that conflict | ≤3 outcomes and ≤3 priorities, already in the spec. Add a light "does this compete for the same hours?" cue when the planned hours for all outcomes exceed free hours. | #15–#17 |

## 4. Critique of the current spec-review design

**Keep**
- One flow with a start, steps and a done state; resumable through `week.step`.
- Review ends in a plan; ≤3 outcomes; "Not yet / Dropped" choices. Dropping is supported by #18; make it feel neutral, not like failure.
- Evidence (linked tasks + time) before the judgment.
- Optional, non-blocking day check-in; the Skip state.

**Change**
1. **No daily planning ritual exists.** The spec covers the evening check-in but not the morning plan, the step with the most direct evidence (#1, plus prior implementation-intentions research). Add §2's day steps 1–4 as a light "Plan today" header on Today, with no extra screen.
2. **Add "Clear the deck" to the weekly review** (GTD get clear/current). Today the review judges outcomes but never touches overdue tasks, the inbox or stale projects. That is the main way the system decays.
3. **Merge step 3 "The week" into steps 2 and 5.** A read-only data page is the likeliest place for people to drop out (the spec's own risk). Show plan-vs-tracked per outcome in step 2 and next week's fixed commitments in step 5.
4. **Cut Reflect to one required-looking prompt** ("One change for next week"). Make "What worked" and "What fell short" optional and collapsed.
5. **Plan step: each outcome gets a first task and a day.** That turns the outcome into an if-then plan; the spec only caps the number of outcomes.
6. **Carry limit:** after 2 carries, require shrink / re-date / drop (#18, #20). The spec's default lets an outcome carry forever.
7. **Quarter wrap-up: do not pre-fill unfinished priorities.** Ask Keep / Change / Drop instead. Add a midpoint check in week 6–7 (#11, #24).
8. **Review day is configurable** (Friday per Vanderkam, Sunday per Sunsama, Monday as now). The "done by Tuesday" target should follow the user's chosen day. Add the quick-review path for missed weeks.
9. **Change the "about 5 min" label** to "about 10 min". Six steps plus deck-clearing will not fit in 5; a too-low estimate breaks trust the first time. Measure the real time from `week.step` timestamps.

**Cut / defer**
- "Also finished" (unlinked done tasks): keep it but collapse it. It matters for morale (Wodtke's Friday wins, #11), not for decisions.
- Time by project as a full breakdown: show the top 3 plus "untracked". Minute-level variances are noise. Useful signals: planned vs actual hours on outcomes, estimate bias by task type (#3), and share of time on the user's chosen categories (#12, #23).

**Metrics to add to §7:** median review duration; share of weeks reviewed within 7 days; overdue count after each review (should fall); outcomes carried ≥ 2.

## 5. Gaps

- No RCT or field study found on weekly reviews, daily planning rituals, review frequency or duration, or why people skip reviews.
- No study comparing night-before and morning planning.
- No research on "to-do list bankruptcy", task aging or auto-rollover vs explicit carry-over.
- Parke 2018 effect sizes and the exact definition of contingent planning; full text not read.
- Bandura & Schunk effect sizes (scanned PDF); Gray et al. 2017 r = .34 seen only in a blog.
- Riddell 2025 pooled effects not seen; findings come from press coverage and the abstract.
- 12 Week Year's 85% and its claimed link between execution score and outcomes are unverified in the book.
- Things 3 rollover behaviour is unconfirmed; Sunsama's default auto-archive threshold is not documented.
- Not searched: ATUS methods for self-tracking; Guillebeau's annual review; Wodtke's full four-square layout (top-left and bottom-left squares unconfirmed).
- Evidence on an individual's number of active projects (vs goal conflict) is indirect; the cap of 3 is a practitioner number.
