# Productivity methods · Evidence review for skkil-time

_Retrieved 2026-10-23 · Staleness horizon: 180 days · Scope: which planning/review/focus rituals have real evidence_

Evidence grades: **A** meta-analysis / RCT / peer-reviewed replication · **B** single peer-reviewed study or reputable survey · **C** practitioner/book/vendor · **D** forum/anecdote. Numbers marked _(secondary)_ were seen only in summaries, not the primary text.

## 2. Per-method notes

### Implementation intentions (A)
- Claim: "If situation X, then I do Y" plans raise follow-through.
- Sources: Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, _Adv. Exp. Soc. Psych._ 38:69–119, 94 tests, d = .65 ([KOPS record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f)). Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2025, _Eur. Rev. Soc. Psych._ 36(1):162–194, 642 tests, .27 ≤ d ≤ .66; larger when the plan is in if-then format, motivation is high, and plans are rehearsed ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)). Field RCT: Milkman et al. 2011 PNAS, writing date **and time** raised flu vaccination by 4.2 pp; date only +1.5 pp (n.s.) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3127912)).
- Replication: very large literature; the 2025 meta is by the original authors. Pooled d for the 2025 meta not verified.
- Caveat: rigid specific plans can make people miss better alternatives (Masicampo & Baumeister 2012, _Social Cognition_ 30:37–55; [author page](https://roybaumeister.com/2012/06/16/committed-but-closed-minded-when-making-a-specific-plan-for-a-goal-hinders-success/)).
- Product: Make "when + where/trigger" a first-class field on a task, not just a due date. Date-only is not enough.

### Self-monitoring of goal progress (A)
- Source: Harkin et al. 2016, _Psych. Bull._ 142(2):198–229. 138 RCTs, N = 19,951. Monitoring frequency d+ = 1.98; goal attainment d+ = .40 [.32, .48]. Larger effects when information was **physically recorded** and when outcomes were **reported or made public** ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/)). Sub-group d values (e.g., .47 vs .19) are _secondary_, unverified.
- Concerns: mostly health behaviors; moderator tests are between-study.
- Product: Intention-vs-reality capture is evidence-backed, but the user must see and record it. Make the review a visible artifact with an optional share/report step, not a silent log.

### Goal setting (A)
- Locke & Latham 2002, _Am. Psych._: specific difficult vs "do your best" d = .42–.80; goal difficulty d = .52–.82 ([PDF](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf)). Epton, Currie & Armitage 2017, _J. Consult. Clin. Psych._: d = .34 [.28, .41], 141 RCT papers, N = 16,523; stronger when goals are difficult, public, or group ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29189034/)).
- Product: Weekly plan should ask for a specific outcome per goal, not a vague theme.

### Planning fallacy and estimate correction (A phenomenon / B fixes)
- Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, _JPSP_: predictions too optimistic across tasks; thesis study mean 33.9 d predicted vs 55.5 d actual, ~30% on time _(figures secondary)_ ([PDF](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf)).
- Unpacking: Kruger & Evans 2004, _JESP_ 40(5):586–594 — listing sub-steps lengthens estimates; one source argues it removes bias rather than increasing accuracy (URL unverified).
- Memory-bias account: Roy, Mitten & Christenfeld 2008 — feedback on past durations improved accuracy across 3 experiments ([record](https://jayscholar.etown.edu/facpubharvest/525)). Thomas & König 2018 — duration feedback did **not** reduce bias; task similarity did ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00760/pdf)).
- Reference-class forecasting (Kahneman/Flyvbjerg) not separately verified in this pass → Gaps.
- Product: Show "you estimated X, similar past tasks took Y" at estimate time (reference class from the user's own actuals). Evidence says this is plausible, not proven; A/B test it.

### Zeigarnik, Ovsiankina, and plan-making (A against / B)
- Ghibellini & Meier 2025, _Humanit. Soc. Sci. Commun._ 12: meta-analysis, **no memory advantage for unfinished tasks**; general tendency to **resume** them ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)).
- Masicampo & Baumeister 2011, _JPSP_ 101(4):667–683: unfulfilled goals caused intrusions/interference; writing a specific plan eliminated them ([PDF](https://users.wfu.edu/masicaej/MasicampoBaumeister2011JPSP.pdf)). No replication found. Baumeister-lab work has been hit by replication problems elsewhere (ego depletion); treat as B.
- Product: "Park it with a plan" (next step + when) is a reasonable capture flow. Don't market "Zeigarnik" claims.

### Attention residue, switching, interruptions (A / B)
- Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans 2001, _JEP:HPP_ 27(4):763–797: switch costs rise with complexity and unfamiliarity ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.27.4.763)).
- Leroy 2009, _OBHDP_ 109(2):168–181: attention residue after switching; finishing under time pressure reduced residue ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2009.04.002)).
- Leroy & Glomb 2018, _Organization Science_: a brief "ready-to-resume" note at interruption reduced residue and improved performance on the interrupting task; 4 studies ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180116151600.htm)). Primary text not read.
- Mark, Gudith & Klocke 2008, CHI: interrupted people finished in less time with no quality loss but more stress, frustration, effort ([PDF](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Mark-CHI08.pdf)). González & Mark 2005 (ECSCW): ~10–12 working spheres/day, ~10.5–12 min per sphere before switching ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/1-4020-4023-7_8)). The popular "23 min 15 s to resume" figure: primary source not verified.
- Product: On every switch/stop, prompt a 1-line "where I stopped / next step" note. Cheap, evidence-backed, fits time capture.

### Multitasking (Ophir 2009) (A against)
- Ophir, Nass & Wagner 2009 PNAS reported heavy media multitaskers more distractible ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19706386)). Wiradhany & Nieuwenstein 2017: 2 replications + meta of 39 tests, pooled d = .17, non-significant after small-study correction ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5662702)).
- Product: Use lab switch-cost evidence (above), not "multitaskers are broken" claims.

### Self-tracking framing (B)
- Kim et al. 2016 CHI "TimeAware": 8-week deployment, N = 24; only the group shown **distracting** time improved productivity ([PDF](https://terpconnect.umd.edu/~choe/download/CHI-2016-Kim-TimeAware.pdf)).
- Product: Test showing "time lost vs plan" framing; small study, watch for guilt/churn.

### Reflection / weekly review / shutdown (B− / C)
- Di Stefano, Gino, Pisano & Staats 2014, HBS WP 14-093: call-center trainees who reflected 15 min at day's end scored 22.8% higher on the final test; effect mediated by self-efficacy ([HBS WK](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/reflecting-on-work-improves-job-performance), [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/wpaper/hal-01970756.html)). No journal version found. Co-author Gino had other papers retracted in 2023 after data-integrity allegations; this paper is not one I found implicated, but independent replication matters.
- Newport shutdown ritual (_Deep Work_, 2016): no study found. Anecdotal (C).
- Weekly review specifically: no direct study found; supported indirectly by self-monitoring (#2) and goal setting (#3).
- Product: Keep a short daily/weekly reflection prompt ("what did I learn / what's next"), 5–15 min max. Don't claim the shutdown ritual is proven.

### Breaks, Pomodoro, ultradian (A / B / D)
- Albulescu et al. 2022, PLOS ONE: micro-breaks (≤10 min) vigor d = .36, fatigue d = .35, performance d = .16 (n.s.); longer breaks → bigger performance boost ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c6f37b6ba159469296b2cb968b65505f)).
- Biwer et al. 2023, _BJEP_ 93(S2):353–367, N = 87 students: self-regulated breaks → longer sessions and breaks, more fatigue and distraction, lower concentration and motivation; same task completion → systematic breaks more efficient ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/), [Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/understanding-effort-regulation-comparing-pomodoro-breaks-and-sel)). Smits, Wenzel & de Bruin 2025 preprint: Pomodoro vs Flowtime vs self-regulated, no differences ([preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202503.0845/v1)) — not peer-reviewed.
- Ultradian 90-min work cycles: Kleitman's BRAC is clearest in sleep; daytime work-block claim has no trial found (D).
- Product: Offer optional fixed-interval break nudges; frame as energy/fatigue, not output. No "90-minute rhythm" feature.

### Urgency vs importance (A for bias; C for Eisenhower matrix)
- Zhu, Yang & Hsee 2018, _JCR_ 45(3):673–690: people choose urgent low-payoff tasks over non-urgent higher-payoff ones ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/45/3/673/4847790)). Mills, Cubitt & Starmer 2026, _JCR_ (online 2026-09-18): independent replication, n = 2,374, effect significant in all tests but **attenuated** with added controls ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/jcr/ucag026/8817231)).
- Eisenhower matrix as a tool: no test found (C).
- Product: Surface importance/payoff next to deadlines when the user picks today's tasks; nudge against deadline-only sorting.

### Few daily priorities, WIP limits (D / B−)
- Ivy Lee "6 tasks": origin story undocumented ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2022/09/01/dark-history-executive-to-do-list-productivity-history-jim-edwards)).
- Little's law (avg WIP = throughput × lead time) is a queueing identity, not a behavior finding. Sjøberg 2018 (ESEM), 5 Kanban teams, 8,000+ items: lower WIP ↔ shorter lead time, but WIP also correlated with productivity ([SINTEF](https://www.sintef.no/publikasjoner/publikasjon/1637884/)). Ahmad et al. 2018 mapping study: Kanban research mostly descriptive ([Oulu](https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/handle/10024/24626)).
- Product: A soft cap on "today" items and in-progress items is reasonable (indirect: planning fallacy + lead time), but present it as a default, not science.

### Time blocking / timeboxing (C)
- No controlled trial comparing calendar-blocking to to-do lists found. Indirect support: implementation intentions and Milkman 2011 date+time effect.
- Counter-evidence for leisure: Tonietto & Malkoc 2016, _JMR_ 53(6), 13 studies: scheduling leisure makes it feel like work and reduces enjoyment; "rough" scheduling without fixed times removes the penalty ([PDF](https://www.business.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/documents/tonietto-the-calendar-mindset.pdf)).
- Product: Time blocking for work tasks = OK (if-then mechanism). Allow "rough" blocks (morning/afternoon) and never auto-schedule leisure to the minute.

### Habits, fresh starts, temptation bundling (B)
- Lally et al. 2010, _EJSP_ 40:998–1009: 96 people, 84 days; 66-day median is for the 39 with good model fit; 18–254 d range, upper end extrapolated ([UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)). Singh et al. 2024, _Healthcare_ 12(23):2488: 20 studies, N = 2,601; only 4 report timing; medians 59–66 d, means 106–154 d, range 4–335 d ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014, _Mgmt Sci_: "diet" searches, gym visits, goal commitments rise after temporal landmarks ([PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)). No replication found; one dissertation suggests it holds only for self-chosen start dates ([egrove](https://egrove.olemiss.edu/etd/2026)).
- Milkman, Minson & Volpp 2014, _Mgmt Sci_: temptation bundling at gym; effect declined over time ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v60y2014i2p283-299.html)). Kirgios et al. 2020 OBHDP follow-up (URL not verified).
- Product: Don't show streak promises like "21 days". Offer "fresh start" re-planning on Mondays/month starts; let users pick the date.

### Flow / deep work (B / C)
- Liu et al. 2023 meta of work flow antecedents (k = 113, N ≈ 60,000) ([TU Delft](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/antecedents-and-outcomes-of-work-related-flow-a-meta-analysis)) — figures secondary. Flow–performance link exists but causal direction unclear (Harris et al., secondary). No empirical test of "Deep Work" found.
- Product: Protected focus blocks are fine; don't claim measured output gains.

### Meeting-free days (C)
- Laker, Pereira, Budhwar & Malik 2022, MIT SMR: 76 companies, pulse surveys; "best results" at 3 meeting-free days/week; widely cited "+71% productivity" is self-reported ([MIT SMR](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-surprising-impact-of-meeting-free-days/)). Not peer-reviewed.
- Product: Maker/manager split is a fine template; cite as practitioner evidence.

### GTD (C)
- Heylighen & Vidal 2008: theoretical alignment with cognitive science, no test ([PDF](https://web-archive.southampton.ac.uk/cogprints.org/6289/1/Heylighen-Vidal-GTD-Science.pdf)). Crucial Learning (vendor) survey of 2,072 people: vendor-run, not peer-reviewed ([blog](https://cruciallearning.com/blog/how-to-manage-it-all/)).
- Product: Borrow capture → next action; skip the full GTD ontology (contexts, someday lists) as a default.

### ADHD externalizing time (D)
- No systematic review or RCT found for visual timers or body doubling; an HCI paper calls body-doubling effectiveness "elusive" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153)).
- Product: Visible timers/co-working can be offered as options; no efficacy claims.

### To-do apps backfiring (C / D)
- No peer-reviewed study found showing long lists cause abandonment. arXiv study of a research group: up to 17% of tasks abandoned/incomplete; deadline tasks done faster ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.16574)). "41% never done": vendor book (D).
- Aeon, Faber & Panaccio 2021 PLOS ONE meta: time management relates moderately to performance, **more** to wellbeing/life satisfaction ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7799745/)).
- "Productivity theater" / planning-as-procrastination: no direct study found → Gaps.

## 3. Product implications

**Build (A-grade backing)**
1. **If-then scheduling**: each planned task gets a when (time block or trigger) and where/context. Date-only is weaker (Milkman 2011).
2. **Intention vs reality review**: auto-captured actuals shown against plan, with the user actively confirming/recording it; optional share/accountability (Harkin 2016 moderators).
3. **Specific outcomes per weekly goal**, kept few and challenging (Locke & Latham; Epton).
4. **Estimate calibration**: show the user's own historical actuals for similar tasks at estimate time; nudge unpacking big tasks into steps (B, test it).
5. **Stop/switch note**: one-line "where I stopped, next step" on every switch or interruption (Leroy & Glomb 2018).
6. **Importance beside urgency** in daily pick to counter the mere urgency effect.

**Offer, frame carefully (B)**
- Optional fixed-interval breaks (fatigue/mood, not output). Short end-of-day reflection (≤15 min). Fresh-start replans on user-chosen landmarks. "Time lost vs plan" framing as an experiment.

**Don't build or don't claim (C/D or refuted)**
- No "Zeigarnik" or "21-day habit" marketing; no "90-minute ultradian" mode; no claims that GTD, shutdown rituals, Ivy Lee, Pomodoro, meeting-free days or deep work are proven.
- Don't hard-schedule leisure/breaks to the minute (Tonietto & Malkoc).
- Avoid over-rigid plans with no re-plan path (Masicampo & Baumeister 2012).

## 4. Gaps (not verified in this pass)
- Pooled d for Sheeran et al. 2025 (642 tests); sub-group d values in Harkin 2016.
- Reference-class forecasting evidence (Kahneman & Tversky; Flyvbjerg) for personal tasks; whether tracking actuals improves personal estimates in field settings.
- Mark's "23 min 15 s" resumption figure: primary source not located.
- Any replication of Masicampo & Baumeister 2011, Dai et al. 2014, Leroy 2009, or Di Stefano et al.
- Effect sizes in the 2026 mere-urgency replication (paywalled).
- Temptation-bundling megastudy results; Kirgios et al. 2020 details.
- Direct RCTs on time blocking, weekly reviews, shutdown rituals, WIP limits for individuals, overlong to-do lists, "planning as procrastination".
- ADHD time-perception interventions: no PubMed search run; evidence level unknown.
- Liu et al. 2023 flow meta numbers and Harris et al. flow–performance review: secondary only.
