_Retrieved 2026-11-01. ## 2. Cross-cutting patterns

1. **Decide in advance, at several horizons.** Bandiera's CEOs, Porter–Nohria's CEOs (75% scheduled ahead, quarterly agenda), Newport (quarter, week, evening-before), Franklin ("Contrive day's business"). Planned share is the only behaviour that correlated with firm performance in Bandiera et al. (B).
2. **Protect contiguous blocks; consolidate rather than add time.** Drucker's "consolidate," Graham's maker half-days, Perlow's quiet mornings, Darwin's morning block, the CEOs' fragmented alone time (a problem, not a model). The common unit is a half-day or a ≥1-hour block, not a 25-minute slice.
3. **Few active priorities, held by a fixed limit.** Burkeman (closed list ≤10, serialize 1–2 projects), Shape Up appetite, Franklin's one virtue per week, the Porter–Nohria quarterly agenda. Ivy Lee and Buffett say the same but are apocryphal.
4. **Fixed time, flexible scope.** Shape Up appetites, Newport's blocks that he redraws, Burkeman deciding "what to fail at." Time is the constraint; scope adjusts.
5. **A short close-out ritual, every day.** Newport's shutdown, Franklin's evening question and "Examination of the day," Kahneman's DRM (structured recall of yesterday), Amabile's progress focus. They share three steps: capture loose ends, note what moved, set tomorrow.
6. **A weekly reset.** GTD Weekly Review, Newport's Monday plan, Franklin's weekly virtue rotation. A week is the shortest span where priorities get re-chosen; a day only gets scheduled.
7. **Progress and control feel better than volume.** Amabile (progress on 76% of best days, secondary), Meyer et al. (agency, day going as planned). A good day is "moved what matters and stayed in control," not "did many tasks."
8. **Track time briefly and honestly, then act on it.** Drucker's record step, Franklin's dots, Perlow's logs, the CEO diaries. In every case logging was a diagnostic phase of days or weeks, not a permanent burden. Franklin himself quit.

## 3. Contradictions

- **How rigid should the schedule be?** Gates (reported 5-min slots) and Newport ("every minute" blocked) against Graham and Darwin (a few big blocks) and Burkeman (accept finitude, don't optimise). Newport resolves it by redrawing blocks freely. Franklin couldn't hold a fixed schedule once others controlled his hours. The earlier review found scheduling leisure turns it into work (B).
- **Meetings: bad or good?** Graham and Perlow treat them as fragmentation. Bandiera's better-matched "leader" CEOs hold long, planned, multi-function meetings, and Meyer et al. find meetings constructive in planning phases. What hurts is unplanned interaction at the wrong time, not meetings as such.
- **Capture everything vs limit the list.** GTD wants every commitment captured and reviewed. Burkeman says the open list will be "nightmarishly long" and only the closed list counts. Both can hold if capture is cheap and commitment is capped.
- **Streaks vs forgiveness.** "Don't break the chain" is apocryphal. Franklin made his tracking lighter over time and then stopped. Habit timelines vary widely (Lally, in the earlier review), so streak-loss framing has no support from these sources.
- **Individual discipline vs social contract.** Perlow shows quiet time decayed without shared incentives. Newport and Graham assume a solo person in control. For a single-user app, only the user's own commitment holds the block up.
- **Ideal deep-work dose.** Pang's ~4 h ceiling (C/D) against CEOs, who rarely get any long block. There's no consensus number. Don't hard-code one.

## 4. Implications for the planner's day / week / quarter flow

**Day (Today view)**
- **Plan the night before or first thing, in under 10 minutes** (Newport spends 10–20 min). Ask: what's fixed (calendar), what is the 1–3 most important (outcome-linked) work, and which block does it go in. Keep it to a small set, not a full Todoist dump.
- **One-tap "replan the rest of today"** that rebuilds the remaining blocks when the day breaks. Show broken plans as normal, not as failure. Newport's blank column is the model.
- **Default block size of 60–120 min for focus work**, with a "protected" flag. Warn softly when a planned interruption (a meeting or short task) would split a protected block. That's Perlow's and Graham's lesson.
- **Shutdown as the day check-in's second half.** Three steps, ~2 min: (1) inbox count to zero or deferred, (2) "what moved today?" pre-filled from completed tasks and tracked time on outcome-linked work, (3) a nudge to roll unfinished items to tomorrow. Then a "Shutdown complete" state. The current felt-rating plus one-line note fits Amabile and DRM; add a progress line next to it.
- **Show progress, not volume.** Lead with "moved: outcome X, 2 h on Project Y" rather than "14/20 tasks done." Setbacks weigh more than progress, so don't greet the user with red overdue counts.

**Week (/review/$week)**
- **Keep the review to Allen's three phases**, mapped to existing steps: Clear (inbox to zero, loose captures), Current (last week's outcomes: achieved/carried/dropped; next 2 weeks of calendar; waiting-for), Creative (pick ≤3 outcomes; someday list). Show the time remaining and let the user finish early.
- **Planned vs actual, from time entries and blocks.** One chart: share of tracked time on outcome-linked work versus the rest (the Porter–Nohria "43% on agenda" idea, secondary). One number, no dashboard.
- **Drop is a normal action.** Burkeman's "decide what to fail at" fits the existing dropped status. Make it as easy as carry.
- **Weekly template of protected blocks** (Newport's "simple rules," Perlow's quiet mornings): let the user set recurring focus windows once and have the week inherit them.

**Quarter**
- **3–5 quarter priorities, hard-capped**, each with an appetite in weeks (Shape Up) rather than an estimate. Weekly outcomes must link to a priority or be marked "maintenance." That's PARA's project vs area split and Bandiera's planned agenda.
- **Quarter health = weeks with an outcome toward each priority**, not task counts.
- **Treat 13 weeks as the cycle**, as Franklin did. It's a natural fresh start (fresh-start effect, earlier review).

**Habits / tracking**
- **No streak-loss shaming.** Franklin's dots recorded lapses without resets, and he lightened the practice over time. Use "x of last 7/30 days" or a forgiving chain with skips allowed.
- **Time tracking as a diagnostic, not a chore.** Offer "track a normal week" (Drucker's record step) and summarise it in the weekly review. Don't require a running timer every day.

**Avoid**
- Marketing claims built on Ivy Lee, Buffett 25/5, Seinfeld chains or Gates/Musk 5-minute slots (grade D).
- A mandated Pomodoro or 90-minute length. Let the user set the block length.

## 5. Gaps (not verified)

- Porter & Nohria original HBR text (paywalled). The 72/28, 75% scheduled and 43%-on-agenda figures are secondary. The exact share of alone time in ≤1 h blocks (59% in one low-quality source) is unconfirmed.
- Amabile & Kramer 76% / 67% / 5% figures come from secondary sources only; the book and the HBR full text weren't reached.
- Meyer et al. full-paper numbers on good vs typical days (PDF returned 403). Only the abstract-level findings above are verified.
- Gloria Mark CHI 2005: 10.5 min and 57% come from the abstract and summaries. The "23 min 15 s to resume" is from a Fast Company interview, not the paper.
- Newport's own multi-scale or quarterly planning post wasn't found on calnewport.com. The quarterly layer is verified only via his "plan every minute" post mentioning quarterly notes.
- Drucker's verbatim "consolidate" wording comes from excerpts and summaries. Grove's personal calendar practices weren't found.
- Burkeman's closed-list cap (≤10) and "serialize" are from secondary summaries of the book.
- Buffett 25/5: no primary confirmation or denial exists, only Flint's account. Seinfeld's denial is relayed by Clear, and the primary Seinfeld quote wasn't located.
- Kahneman DRM: the *Science* paper text wasn't retrieved, so findings are from press releases.
- No study found that directly compares high performers' planning horizons with average workers' outcomes outside the CEO literature. The CEO findings may not transfer to an individual planner user.
