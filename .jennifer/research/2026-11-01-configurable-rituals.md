# Configurable rituals (zen/review mode)

2026-11-01. Grades: **A** meta-analysis/RCT/systematic review · **B** single peer-reviewed study · **C** practitioner/vendor · **D** anecdote. _(secondary)_ = the figure comes from a summary, not the paper. Builds on `2026-11-01-why-planners-fail.md` (Stawarz 2015, Kocielnik 2018, Meyer 2017) and `2026-11-01-rituals-and-recovery.md` (Scullin, Parke, Sunsama, GTD), which are not repeated here.

**Bottom line:** The strongest evidence supports two small rituals. The first is a **short debrief after a work session** (debrief meta-analysis, d ≈ .67). The second is a **specific to-do list written before sleep** (Scullin). No study compares morning planning with evening planning, and chronotype barely predicts cognitive performance. So the app should not pick a "right" planning time. It should ship a few templates, let the user choose *when* each one runs, and keep the step list short and fixed. The bedtime ritual conflicts with screen advice. The current evidence points to keeping it short and non-interactive and running it before bed, not in bed.

## 2. Ritual catalog

| Ritual | Steps (in order) | Target time | Default trigger | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Session close** (after a focus block or timer stop) | 1. Did it move? (done / partly / no) · 2. Next step: one line, the ready-to-resume note · 3. Breathing pause, optional, about 30 s | **≤ 60 s** | Stopping a timer longer than 20 min. Skippable with one key | A (#1, #2) for debriefing in general; B (#3) for the resume note. No study of solo micro-debriefs after Pomodoros |
| **Morning start** | 1. Feel (1 tap) · 2. Leftovers triage (keep / reschedule / drop) · 3. Pick 1–3 musts · 4. Time-block them | 2–5 min | First open of the app each day | B (Parke, in prior file) for daily planning; Sunsama/Akiflow pattern (C) |
| **Evening shutdown + plan tomorrow** | 1. What moved (auto-listed) · 2. Leftovers triage · 3. Pick tomorrow's musts · 4. Time-block tomorrow, optional · 5. One-line note | 3–7 min | User-set time (default 17:30), or after the day's last time block ends | B (#5, #6): writing open loops down specifically lowers arousal. No head-to-head study with morning planning |
| **Wind-down** | 1. Brain-dump tomorrow's to-dos, prompting for specifics (#5) · 2. Habit check (only habits due tonight) · 3. Closing screen: "Put the device away" | **≤ 5 min** | Bedtime minus 30–60 min, opt-in only | B (#5) for the list; the screen caveat rests on B (#7, #8). See the conflict note below |
| **Weekly review** | Existing `/review/$week` flow (prior file) | 15–30 min | User-chosen day | C (GTD/Sunsama); see `rituals-and-recovery.md` |

**Wind-down vs screen advice.** The only lab evidence of harm (#7) used 4 h of reading on a bright tablet. In free-living conditions, 2 h of screen use before bed was mostly neutral, while **interactive** use and use **in bed** were what hurt (#8). The behaviour Scullin tested was 5 min of writing. Design implications: 5 min or less, a dark low-luminance theme with the water animation slowed or frozen, typing only (no browsing tasks, no time-blocking), a hard stop screen at the end, and a nudge to run the ritual before getting into bed. Users who plan in the evening should get the **shutdown** early in the evening (as in #6) and the wind-down kept as an optional extra, not the other way round.

**Chronotype implication.** Evidence for a "best time to plan" does not exist (#10, prior file #2). Morningness tracks conscientiousness (#12), so the people who most need a planning nudge are less likely to be morning types. A morning-only default would fit the users who need it least. **Default:** onboarding asks one question, "When do you plan your day? Morning / Evening", and turns on Morning start **or** Evening shutdown, never both. Session close is on for everyone, and Weekly review stays on. Wind-down stays off until the user enables it.

**Pattern.** Planners (Sunsama, Akiflow, Ellie) use **fixed steps with a configurable time**. Routine apps (Routinery, Tiimo) use **full step builders** but serve a different job: physical routines run on timers. The 173-vote Sunsama request shows that users want to choose and reorder steps. No planner offers that, and nothing shows whether it would improve adherence. The useful idea from Sunsama and Akiflow is that a skipped ritual **folds into the next one** instead of nagging.

## 4. Recommended model

**Ritual = trigger + ordered steps (from a fixed library) + on/off.** Nothing else.

- **Ritual:** `kind` (one of the five templates, so the ritual can be duplicated but has no free-form type), `enabled`, `trigger`, `steps[]` (ordered step keys), `last_completed_at`.
- **Trigger** (choose one): `first_open` · `at_time(HH:MM, days)` · `after_last_block` · `after_timer_stop(min_minutes)` · `manual`.
- **Step library** (closed set, each step a ready-made screen that writes to existing tables): `feel` (day check-in) · `what_moved` (auto-list of done tasks and tracked time) · `leftovers_triage` · `pick_musts` (1–3) · `timeblock` (today or tomorrow, inferred from the trigger) · `one_line_note` · `habit_check` (due habits only) · `breathing_pause` (fixed 30–60 s) · `next_step_note` (writes the resume note on the task) · `brain_dump` (to Inbox, prompting for specifics) · `outcomes` (weekly) · `device_away` (closing screen for wind-down).

**Five built-in templates** (§2): Morning start, Evening shutdown, Wind-down, Session close, Weekly review. Users can toggle steps within a template and reorder them. They cannot write custom prompts in v1.

**Behaviour rules (not options):**
- A skipped or missed ritual folds into the next one: missed shutdown leads to leftovers triage at the start of Morning start. There is no catch-up nag (Sunsama/Akiflow pattern; #15).
- Every ritual has an always-visible "Skip" and exits in one key. Snooze is fixed at 15 min, at most once.
- Session close appears only after sessions of 20 min or more. One prompt per session, never stacked.
- Rotate the wording of `feel`, `one_line_note` and `what_moved` from a small fixed pool. Repetitive prompts are what kill reflection (Kocielnik, prior file).
- Trigger rituals from events (first open, timer stop, last block) by default. Use clock times only where no event exists. Event cues build automaticity and reminders do not (Stawarz, prior file).
- Wind-down always uses a dark, low-motion theme and ends on `device_away`.

**Options to hide:**
- Custom free-text steps or prompts (Kocielnik shows prompt *quality* matters; user-written prompts would decay into repetition).
- Per-step timers or durations (a routine-app concept that turns reflection into a chore).
- Several rituals of the same kind, or more than one daily planning ritual. Hidden because planning twice a day is the overplanning failure mode.
- Snooze length, reminder sound, notification frequency.
- Streaks or scores for rituals (prior file: streak loss demotivates).
- Water backdrop or theme per ritual (the backdrop is one global setting).
- Session-close threshold (fixed at 20 min).

## 5. Gaps

- **No study compares planning in the morning with planning in the evening.** The defaults rest on a user question, not on evidence.
- No study tests a solo, sub-minute debrief after each Pomodoro. The effect sizes in #1 and #2 come from team and training debriefs that were longer and usually facilitated.
- No adult consensus on screens before bed (#9). Brosnan studied youth. Effects of a 5-minute dark-mode writing task on sleep are untested.
- I could not verify Ultraworking's exact cycle questions (the site redirects to an unrelated domain), Session's exact prompt wording, or the names of Sunsama's shutdown steps.
- Di Stefano's 23% and Keiser & Arthur's d = 0.79 are secondary figures.
- No evidence on whether *configurable* rituals improve adherence compared with fixed ones. The 173 Sunsama votes show demand, not efficacy.
- Carney's constructive-worry timing ("2 h before bed") is practitioner-sourced.
