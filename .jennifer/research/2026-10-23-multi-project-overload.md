# Multi-project overload: research, AI-era voices, methods, tools

Date: 2026-10-23 · ~20 searches/fetches · Grades: A peer-reviewed/official, B reputable press, C vendor/practitioner, D forum. "(snippet)" = search summary only, full text not read.

## Bottom line

- **Holding many goals is not the problem. Conflict between them and failing to drop dead ones are.** Goal conflict goes with distress, but the effect is inconsistent across studies (Gray et al. 2017; Frontiers 2021). Being able to *disengage* from unattainable goals and re-engage elsewhere is consistently adaptive (Wrosch 2003; Barlow et al. meta-analysis).
- **Switching projects leaves "attention residue."** A one-minute "ready-to-resume" note at the switch measurably reduces it (Leroy 2009; Leroy & Glomb 2018). This is the best-evidenced, cheapest intervention found, and it suits a planner.
- **Without help, people give time to the deadline closest at hand, not the most important goal** (Schmidt & DeShon 2007; Ballard et al. 2018). Students juggling courses, clubs and job applications will let urgent work crowd out important work unless something makes that tradeoff visible.
- **AI-era signal is anecdotal but consistent.** Agents cut the cost of starting ("never accumulated half finished projects at a more impressive rate"). Running parallel agents also drains attention (Willison, Osmani). Counter-voices say agents help them *finish* or revive old projects.
- **Most portfolio methods are practitioner advice** (WIP limits, closed lists, kill criteria, appetite/cool-downs, PARA). Their mechanisms line up with the research on goal shielding, disengagement and residue, but none has been tested on individuals.

## Research findings

| Claim | Finding | Grade | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal conflict harms wellbeing | Meta-analysis of goal conflict and psychological well-being (Gray, Ozer & Rosenthal 2017, JRP 66:27–37). A blog reports r=.34 across 54 studies; **unverified**. A 2021 review calls the relationship "not very clear" | A (snippet) | Frontiers 2021 |
| Conflicting strivings → more rumination, less action | Emmons & King 1988 (JPSP 54:1040), as summarized by secondary theses: people with goal conflict think more about their goals and act less on them | A via secondary (snippet) | WDH record |
| Goal progress drives wellbeing | Meta-analysis of 108 samples: successful striving–SWB ρ=.43, stronger for *progress* than for attainment | A (snippet) | J. Happiness Studies 2015 |
| Focus shields against competing goals | Goal shielding: a committed focal goal inhibits competing goals and aids attainment (Shah, Friedman & Kruglanski 2002, 6 studies) | A (snippet) | Duke Scholars |
| Prioritization across goals is dynamic | Time goes to the goal furthest behind, and this depends on time remaining (Schmidt & DeShon 2007). People favor the shorter deadline, against theory (Ballard, Vancouver & Neal 2018). Reviewed in Neal et al. 2017 *Annual Review* | A (snippet) | UMN; Ballard 2018 PDF; DeepDyve |
| Letting go is adaptive | Goal-disengagement and re-engagement capacities relate to wellbeing (Wrosch et al. 2003). Barlow et al. meta-analysis pools 33 samples on health outcomes | A (snippet) | EHP; GAS article |
| Switching leaves residue | Attention stays on the unfinished prior task and the next task suffers (Leroy 2009, OBHDP 109:168–181) | A (snippet) | strategy+business; BPS |
| Residue is reducible | A "ready-to-resume plan" (where I stopped, what's next) significantly lowered residue in 4 studies, including a field survey of 202 professionals | B (university press) | UW News 2018 |
| Too many options paralyze | Choice overload: mean effect ≈0 in Scheibehenne 2010. It is significant under moderators (task difficulty, complexity, preference uncertainty) per Chernev 2015 | A (snippet) | Kellogg; Scheibehenne PDF |
| WIP limits | Team-level only: lower WIP correlates with shorter lead time (5 teams, 8,000+ items). No study gives an optimal limit and none tests individuals | A (snippet) | SINTEF/ACM 2018 |

## Practitioner methods

| Method | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Personal kanban / WIP limit | Caps active work, so switching drops | C. Team data only (A) |
| Burkeman closed list (max 10) + 1–2 big projects at once ("serialize") | Fixed volume. New work enters only when something finishes | C (snippet) |
| Newport *Slow Productivity*: "do fewer things," multi-scale planning | Cuts the "overhead tax" of tracking many commitments | C (secondary) |
| Annie Duke kill criteria ("states and dates") | Sets the quit rule in advance, which puts disengagement research into practice | C (snippet). Underlying disengagement research is A |
| Shape Up appetite + cool-down (6-week cycle, then 2-week cool-down) | Fixed time and variable scope. Cool-down is for cleanup and choosing the next bet | C (snippet) |
| PARA projects vs areas; Things Areas/Projects | Projects have an outcome and a deadline, so they can end. Areas are ongoing | C |
| GTD weekly review, ready-to-resume note | Closes open loops and lowers residue | Resume note: A/B. Weekly review: C |

## Tools

- **Sunsama:** channels label tasks. *Contexts* group channels "so that you can quickly focus your attention on one context at a time." Personal contexts can be excluded from the workload count. It recommends about 5–7 channels per context. Weekly objectives. [C, official help]
- **Things:** Areas for an "ongoing ambition," Projects for multi-step work. [C, official]
- **Motion / Akiflow:** comparison sites describe Motion's AI scheduling by priority, due date and estimate, and call Akiflow's project support "Basic." [C, third-party]
- **Agent-era:** Anthropic's Claude Code Agent View (2026-05-11 preview) groups sessions as Needs input, Ready for review, Working and Completed. Claude Code Projects (beta 2026-09-17) runs one orchestrator per project. Both address sessions, not the human's portfolio of projects. [C]
- No third-party planner aimed at agent-era builders' project portfolios was found.

## Implications for the product (interpretation)

1. **Make switching cheap and clean.** At the end of a block or agent session, prompt a 15-second resume note ("stopped at / next / blocked on"). Show it when the person returns. This is the strongest evidence-backed feature found.
2. **Show conflicts, not just lists.** In the weekly review, show the active projects side by side and flag when this week's blocks all go to the nearest deadline (the Ballard pattern) while a weekly outcome gets none.
3. **Normalize quitting.** On project creation, ask for an optional kill criterion (state + date). In the weekly review, offer Pause / Kill / Continue for projects with no progress. Disengagement research supports making this guilt-free.
4. **Separate projects from areas.** Courses and job search are areas; this week's deliverables are projects. That separation keeps the active-project count honest.
5. **Make agent sessions count toward load.** Count parallel sessions per project as active WIP. Nudge the user when parallel threads exceed a personal ceiling they set themselves (Osmani).
6. **Measure progress, not completion.** Wellbeing tracks progress more than attainment, so show weekly progress per outcome.

## Gaps

- No individual-level trial of WIP limits, project caps or kill criteria.
- No data (only anecdotes) on whether agents raise the number of abandoned projects.
- Effect sizes for Gray 2017 and Barlow 2020 not verified from full text.
- Reddit and X were not reached.
- Linear-for-individuals and Notion were not researched.
- Sunsama, Motion and Akiflow prices were not checked.
